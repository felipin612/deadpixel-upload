## e01
There is a number in the corner of the screen. It says steps behind you: one. I have not moved. I am sitting on my bed with the controller in my lap, and the number just went up to two.

Let me go back to the beginning.

## e02
The disc came in the mail. No return address. A padded envelope with my name written in pencil, and inside it a black PlayStation 2 case with a silver square drawn on the front. If you watched my last video, you know that square. Dead Pixel Studios. A studio that worked from 2001 to 2004 and never released a single game.

There was a sticky note on the case. It said: this one is yours now. Walk slow.

I thought it was a fan. Someone who saw my video and burned me a prank. I almost threw it away. I wish I had.

## e03
The game is called NIGHTWALK. There is no menu music. The title screen is a road at night, a single streetlight, and fog that moves the wrong way, toward the camera instead of away from it. When I pressed start, the screen went black and a line of text came up, white on black, one letter at a time.

You may walk as far as you like. Do not look back.

That was the whole tutorial.

## e04
You play as someone you never see. First person. A lantern in your right hand. The road goes straight ahead into fog, and every forty steps or so there is another streetlight. That is it. No enemies. No music. Just footsteps, and the hiss of the lantern.

I tried to turn the camera with the right stick. Nothing. I tried every button. The camera only looks forward. You can walk, you can stop, and you can pause. That is the entire game.

I walked for twenty minutes the first night. I counted thirty one streetlights. Then the screen faded out on its own and the console reset to the browser. My memory card had a new save. Play time: twenty minutes exactly.

## e05
The second night I noticed the rule card. When you pause, the top of the screen shows a rule, the way old games show a hint. Rule one: do not look back. And under it, a counter I had not seen the first time.

Steps behind you: zero.

I thought it was a step counter that was not finished. Debug text left in a build. Dead Pixel games are full of that. I unpaused and kept walking.

I did try to cheat it, of course. I put the controller down and walked to the TV and looked at the screen from the side, as if the game could not see me there. I held the left stick backwards. The character does not walk backwards. He just stands there, and the fog keeps coming toward him, and after a while you understand that you are not walking down the road. The road is being pulled past you.

## e06
On the third night the counter said four.

I had not done anything different. Same road, same fog, same lantern. But now, when I stopped walking, the footsteps did not stop right away. There was one more. Then another. Soft, a little behind the beat, like someone matching my pace and not quite getting it right.

I turned my TV all the way up and stopped again. One more footstep. Then nothing. Steps behind you: five.

## e07
That night the road ended for the first time. Thirty one streetlights, then a bus stop. A bench, a sign with no name on it, and on the bench, a memory card. A grey PlayStation 2 memory card, sitting there in the game, rendered with more detail than anything else in the world.

When I walked up to it, a text box opened. It said: he waited here for you. He waited here every night.

I did not know who he was. I picked up the card and the game faded out.

Before I picked it up, I stood at that bus stop for almost a minute, because the sign had a timetable. Real numbers, tiny and blurry. Every bus on it left at three thirty three. Every single one. Morning and night.

## e08
The next night the pause screen was different. The rule was still there. Do not look back. But the counter said fourteen, and there was a second line I had never seen. Distance: one step.

I sat there with the game paused for a long time. Paused, the game is silent. And in the silence I heard it in my room. Not from the TV. From the hallway behind my bedroom door. One step. The floorboard by the bathroom that always creaks.

I live alone.

## e09
I went looking for NIGHTWALK online. There is almost nothing. One scan from a 2003 magazine, a preview page that calls it a meditation on loneliness from the tiny team at Dead Pixel. Cancelled, it says, after an incident during testing.

And one comment, on a forum that has been dead for fifteen years. The username is just a string of numbers. It says: the rear camera is still in the debug menu. Don't use it. He is not behind the player. He is behind you.

I spent most of that day reading old Dead Pixel threads. People who found their discs at flea markets, in rental store bins, in the back of a used console they bought for their kids. Every story has the same shape. The game is small. The rule is simple. And the person always breaks the rule, because the game is built so that you will.

Nobody on those forums ever says what happened after. The threads just stop.

## e10
I already knew how to open the debug menu. Same button code as the last Dead Pixel game I found. Hold both shoulder buttons and press select three times. The menu came up in that same green text.

Warp to streetlight. Toggle fog. Free lantern. And at the bottom, an option called rear camera.

I want to tell you I thought about it for a long time. I didn't. I pressed it.

## e11
The camera swung around and for the first time I saw the player character. A grey figure with a hood, holding the lantern, standing in the road. And behind him, maybe ten steps back, another figure. The same shape. The same hood. No lantern.

I switched back to the front camera and walked. I switched to the rear camera again. Now he was five steps back. Front camera. Walk. Rear camera. Two steps. He was not walking when I walked. He was walking when I looked.

On the last switch he was right there, close enough that his hood filled the screen. And the counter in the corner said: steps behind you: one.

## e12
I turned the console off with the power button on the front. I did not save. I sat in the dark and listened.

The floorboard by the bathroom creaked. Then the one in the hallway. Then nothing. I did not turn around. I am telling you that now so you understand. I did not turn around. I watched the black TV screen, because in a black screen you can see what is behind you, and I watched it until the sun came up.

There was nothing in the reflection. Just my room. Just me.

At six in the morning I heard my neighbour leave for work, the door, the stairs, the car. Normal sounds. I have never been so grateful for a car engine in my life. And then, very close, right behind my left shoulder, so close I felt it more than heard it, someone let out a breath. Like they had been holding it for a long time.

## e13
In the morning I turned the game on one more time, to get the disc out cleanly. It did not go to the title screen. It went straight to credits. White text on black, scrolling slowly.

Design, Dead Pixel Studios. Programming, Dead Pixel Studios. And then a name. The first real name I have ever seen in a Dead Pixel game. Quality assurance, T. Rowe. And under it: thank you for walking with us. You can stop now.

T. Rowe. The tester. The man in the newspaper photo, with a dead pixel where his eye should be.

## e14
I looked up the name, of course. There is a T. Rowe in a 2004 local news archive, a short notice, no photo. Reported missing after a night shift. Last seen walking home. The notice does not say which road. It does not say if anyone was behind him.

The disc is back in its black case. The memory card from the bench is on my card, in slot two, a save I never made. Play time: one thousand three hundred and thirty one steps.

I have not turned around since. When I need to leave a room, I walk backwards out of it, so whatever is behind me stays behind me.

If you have ever seen the name T. Rowe on anything, anything at all, tell me in the comments. And if you find a black case with a silver square in your mail, walk slow. Don't look back.
